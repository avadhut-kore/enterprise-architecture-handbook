# Role Profile: The Enterprise Architect

The Enterprise Architect (EA) is a strategic leadership role responsible for ensuring an organization's business strategy, operating model, and technology capabilities evolve in lockstep.

---

## 1. Key Accountabilities
* **Target State Architecture**: Develop and maintain the 3–5 year enterprise target architecture across business, application, data, and technology domains.
* **Transition Roadmap**: Define intermediate architectural transition states that deliver incremental business value while containing delivery risk.
* **Portfolio Rationalization**: Continuously analyze the application and technology portfolio to eliminate redundancy, deprecate legacy systems, and control TCO.
* **Architecture Governance**: Chair or lead the Architecture Review Board (ARB), establishing technology standards, approved reference architectures, and exception processes.
* **Capital Allocation Advisory**: Partner with CIO, CFO, and business executives to evaluate major IT investments during annual and quarterly planning cycles.

## 3. A Day in the Life of an Enterprise Architect

* **Morning**: Reviewing proposed business cases for an upcoming quarterly capital committee; assessing whether a proposed $4M CRM customization can be solved with existing platform capabilities.
* **Mid-Day**: Chairing the Architecture Review Board (ARB) to evaluate a major payment gateway migration; negotiating an exception waiver with security and compliance officers.
* **Afternoon**: Whiteboarding with Business Architects and Product VPs to map the capabilities required for a newly acquired subsidiary.
* **Late Afternoon**: Updating the Enterprise Technology Radar and authoring an Architecture Decision Record (ADR) detailing the enterprise standard for event streaming.
