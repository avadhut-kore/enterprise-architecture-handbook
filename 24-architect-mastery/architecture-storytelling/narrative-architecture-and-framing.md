# Narrative Architecture and Framing

Architects often fail to secure executive funding or engineering passion because they present architecture as dry component blocks rather than a compelling narrative.

## 1. The Story Structure of Architecture

```
[1. The Status Quo & Inciting Incident]
"The company has reached $500M ARR, but our 12-year-old billing core is beginning to buckle..."
                       │
                       ▼
[2. The Antagonist (Systemic Friction & Entropy)]
"Every new pricing model takes 9 months to launch. Our competitors are shipping weekly..."
                       │
                       ▼
[3. The Crossing of the Threshold (The Architectural Vision)]
"We envision a modular, event-driven subscription platform where pricing is code-free..."
                       │
                       ▼
[4. The Ordeal & Trials (Migration & Dual-Run)]
"We will strangle the legacy core safely using event CDC without dropping a single penny..."
                       │
                       ▼
[5. The Promised Land (Target State Transformation)]
"A modern enterprise engine capable of supporting $2B in scale with 99.999% reliability."
```

## 2. Framing Techniques for High Stakeholders

- **Contrast Framing**: Juxtapose the pain of today against the reality of tomorrow (e.g., "Today: 3 AM Sev-1 pages; Tomorrow: Self-healing canary rollbacks").
- **The Metaphor**: Use relatable analogies (e.g., "Replacing the engine on a 747 mid-flight" vs "Rewiring the building's electrical grid while the lights stay on").
